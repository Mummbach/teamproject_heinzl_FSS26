Patient Monitoring Intensity (PMI) as an Additional Predictor

Objective

Objective

Evaluate whether Patient Monitoring Intensity (PMI) improves prediction of prolonged ICU Length of Stay (LOS > 7 days) when incorporated into the baseline GRU model.

Hypothesis

Monitoring intensity reflects clinician assessment of patient severity and therefore provides complementary information beyond physiological measurements.

PMI Features — Capture Aspects of Clinician Assessment Absent From Physiological Signals
Total measurement frequency Mean temporal coverage Maximum monitoring frequency Number of actively monitored variables Mean measurement irregularity Composite PMI score

Why PMI

Physiological time-series describe what the patient's body is doing. PMI describes what the clinical team is doing about it — how often, how broadly, and how consistently the patient is being observed. This is a signal that exists nowhere in the vitals themselves.

Key Findings

  • All major PMI metrics showed statistically significant correlations with LOS
  • Correlations were positive but weak
  • Patients monitored more frequently generally experienced longer ICU stays
  • Measurement irregularity showed a small negative association with LOS
  • PMI alone is not a strong predictor of LOS but contains useful complementary information

Monitoring Reflects Clinician Behavior

Interpretation

Patients Receiving...

More frequent measurements Higher monitoring coverage Greater observation intensity

...are often those considered clinically unstable.

PMI as a Proxy

PMI therefore acts as a proxy for illness severity, perceived clinical risk and intensity of care.

Complementary, Not a Replacement

Rather than replacing physiological variables, PMI complements them by incorporating aspects of clinical decision-making.

Monitoring intensity is not just data collection frequency — it is a recorded trace of clinician judgment, and a measurable complement to physiological signals.
